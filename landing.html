An ant farm you tend

Not an army you drive.

Kingdom is a real-time realm in the old god-view style. You never order a subject around — you raise the works, set a few policies, and the realm runs itself. Every one of your people is a named soul who walks to work, learns a craft over years, eats from the same granary, and panics when the horn sounds. When Berta Stoutheart dies, the Chronicle says so — by name.

The economy and the war are one game: a building's output rises and falls with its hit points, raiders grind those down, and repair competes for the same scarce hands as the harvest. A raid is never an ending — buildings are sacked, not razed — but every wound is measured in lost bread, lost timber, lost years.

How a reign goes

Click a building, click the map. Everything else is your people's work — and your judgment.

Found the realm

Your keep stands, six souls beside it. Raise farms and houses; the border creeps outward around everything you build — and ribbons down every road you lay.

Work the chains

Lumber camps eat the forest, quarries cut stone, ore becomes iron, grain becomes bread. Workers learn by doing — a master doubles a workshop's output, and mastery takes years.

Arm the walls

Walls breach, they don't shatter. A watchtower only shoots while a watchman is posted — and raiders have learned to silence the shield. Your soldiers are subjects under arms; stand them down and they farm as militia.

Weather the raids

Watch the war-camp on the horizon: raiders mass at the tents before they march. Hold the line on home ground where your bonuses live, or sally forth and pay in blood for the loot they carry.

Answer the rider

Before a warlord marches, his rider demands a quarter of your treasury. Pay, and the wave turns away — and the next demand grows. Refuse, and he marches at once. Danegeld is a spiral, not a subscription.

March on the camp

His hoard is a ledger of everything he took from you. Send every sword down the road, break his garrison at the tents — and then, standing in his camp, the game pauses and asks what kind of sovereign you are.

The Three Crowns

Winning is optional. The reign goes on as long as you do.

⚔ RAIDERS APPROACH ⚔

Why it stays interesting

Most city-builders end the day you're safe. This one is only getting started.

Everyone has a name

Deaths are reported by name. Masters carry knowledge that dies with them — lose your only smith and the whole craft dims. Nobody dies at their post: sacked buildings scatter their crews, and the only way a subject falls is caught in the open, on screen, running for the keep.

The map keeps score

Forests are finite: the wood-line visibly recedes under the axes, and spent ground opens into farmland. Camps go quiet in four or five years — so you push the roads out, bridge the rivers, and the border follows. Expansion isn't a score. It's a verb with a cost.

An enemy with an address

The warlord is not weather. He has a camp, a garrison, shepherds and weavers who live in his shadow — and a hoard made of what he took from you. Beat him at the tents and you must choose: burn the war-tents and spare the folk, or leave nothing standing. One survivor always slips through the reeds. The game remembers what you did. So do the men who did it.
